Stock-level index tracking: the results

A benchmark rebuilt month by month from CRSP's list of S&P 500 members, and a long-only mandate held close to it under four covariance estimators from July 1999 to December 2024: its tracking error, what its turnover is made of, and its active return by sector and by factor. Every figure of the mandate is a simulation drawn from the tables committed with the code.

  • Under the mandate the four covariance estimators realise 78.9 to 83.6 basis points a year of tracking error, and each one's forecast understates it. The bias statistic, the standard deviation of the active return in units of each month's forecast, 1 for a calibrated forecast, is 1.48 to 1.85, above the band of 0.8 to 1.25 fixed before the run: the realised active return is 48 to 85 per cent more volatile than forecast.
  • Index changes force 2.99 per cent a year of the Ledoit-Wolf path's 26.26 per cent of one-way turnover, or 11.4 per cent. Loosening the cap on chosen turnover from 0.5 per cent a month to none raises chosen turnover from 6.3 to 59.4 per cent a year on the Ledoit-Wolf estimator and from 6.2 to 14.9 on the factor estimator, and lowers the mean forecast on both. On a paired test, neither estimator's realised tracking error moves by an amount that is statistically significant at the 5 per cent level: plus 3.1 basis points a year on Ledoit-Wolf, with a 95 per cent interval of minus 3.6 to plus 9.8, and minus 3.7 on the factor estimator, from minus 18.9 to plus 11.4, where the two comparisons could detect a change of 9.1 and 17.3 four times in five.
  • Month by month, allocation is the largest of the three Brinson-Fachler terms on the Ledoit-Wolf, factor and principal-component estimators: 16.4 to 16.7 basis points a month in absolute value on average, against 10.6 to 13.8 for selection and 1.5 to 2.2 for interaction. Over the 306 months the active return is 0.2 to 13.7 basis points a year before costs, and on no path is it statistically significant at the 5 per cent level, nor is any of its three terms: each lies within one Newey-West standard error of zero, and a mean active return of about 35 basis points a year in either direction would be needed. The six factors explain 2 to 13 per cent of its variance.
  • The benchmark rebuilt from CRSP's list of index members reproduces CRSP's own value-weighted return of the same list with a tracking error of 11.5 basis points a year from 1926 to 2024. Its momentum and variance spreads have correlations of 0.888 and 0.954 with Kenneth R. French's large-cap spreads.1

Key facts

Data
CRSP and Compustat via Wharton Research Data Services, accessed through Queen Mary University of London, extraction of 28 September 2026. Kenneth R. French Data Library, downloaded at run time.
Period
The benchmark January 1926 to December 2024. The mandate July 1999 to December 2024, 306 months.
Universe
The point-in-time S&P 500 from CRSP's list of index members, about 500 stocks a month.
Benchmark
The cap-weighted universe, at start-of-month market capitalisations.
Mandate
Long-only, each stock and each company outside the tilted sectors and the excluded sub-industries within 50 basis points of its benchmark weight, each untilted sector within 2 percentage points, chosen turnover at most 2 per cent a month, Energy and Utilities stocks between zero and half their benchmark weight, Tobacco and Aerospace and Defense at zero, a beta of one to the benchmark.
Estimators compared
Sample, Ledoit-Wolf, six-factor and principal-component covariances from three years of daily returns.
Rebalancing
Monthly. The turnover that index changes force lies outside the cap.
Costs
Gross of fees. Trading cost at 10 basis points per unit of one-way turnover where stated.
Attribution
By the eleven GICS sectors and Unclassified after Brinson and Fachler (1985)2, and by the five factors of Fama and French (2015)3 and momentum, monthly, summed without linking.
Specification
First committed on 10 November 2026, its 25 levels of gates and expectations unchanged since, and every later change dated in the constants. The author's design notes date it to 9 November 2026, which the repository's history cannot show.

Whether the rebuilt benchmark and the four covariance matrices are what they claim to be.

The universe in a month is the stocks on CRSP's list of index members on the last trading day of the month before, each weighted by its market capitalisation at the start of the month. A delisting is applied by the study's rule, after Shumway (1997)4: CRSP's delisting return where it gives one, and otherwise minus 30 per cent for a delisting code from 400 to 599, which CRSP names a liquidation or a delisting by the exchange5, and zero for any other code. A member's sector is the GICS sector in force on the same day, through CRSP's link to Compustat. The index as S&P publishes it is not the benchmark: its total return is not in the extraction, and CRSP gives one share count per security and no float factor, so the index's float-adjusted weights cannot be rebuilt from it.

Until 2005 the index weighted each stock by its full capitalisation, as the benchmark does, so a member missing from CRSP's list would widen the gap to the index's own price return. From 1957 that gap lies between 20.6 and 36.0 basis points a year in every period. So from 1957 no period stands out, and the gap is no smaller in the years when the index weighted by full capitalisation than after 2005, when it weights by float. The comparison is therefore not fine enough to show the change of weighting, and it would show an error of membership only if the error moved the return by more than these 20 to 36 basis points a year. Before 1957, when the list holds about 90 names, it is 145.8, and the comparison does not confirm the list in those years. Each period's figure is the tracking error pooled over its years.

The months in which the sample matrix's condition number reaches ten times the Ledoit-Wolf one have a cause found after the run: from 2014 the universe holds up to five companies with two share classes each, whose daily returns move almost together, and the difference between two such classes sets the sample matrix's smallest eigenvalue.

How much tracking error each estimator forecast and realised under the mandate, what the turnover cap buys and what the turnover is made of.

Each month the mandate chooses the weights that make the forecast tracking error as small as its constraints allow. Twenty-four paths run from the benchmark over the 306 months: the four estimators under four cumulative constraint sets at the 2 per cent cap, and the Ledoit-Wolf and factor estimators under the mandate at four further caps. Under the first three sets nothing pushes the portfolio off the benchmark, and it stays there in every month. The figures below are the twelve paths under the mandate.

Where the mandate's active return came from, by sector and by factor.

Each month the active return of the four paths at the 2 per cent cap is split by the twelve sectors with the method of Brinson and Fachler (1985)2 into allocation, selection and interaction, which sum to it exactly, and by six factors into contributions and a residual. Both sides are taken over the month's names with a return. A sector with no stock above a weight of one millionth has a selection of zero and keeps the rest of its part as interaction. The monthly terms are summed over months with no geometric linking.

The data, what is known at each date, the costs, what the study cannot show, and the terms.

Method

Data and access. CRSP and Compustat via Wharton Research Data Services, accessed through Queen Mary University of London, from an extraction of 28 September 2026. They are licensed, and the repository holds none of them: it holds code, tests, the extraction's SHA-256 and aggregate tables.6 Nothing that reproduces CRSP's index file is committed by month, and no weight of a security or of a sector of the portfolio is committed in any form. Kenneth R. French's files are downloaded at run time.

What is known at each date. Membership, sector and capitalisation are read on the last trading day of the month before. Each covariance matrix uses the three years of daily returns before the month. Nothing from the month itself enters a weight.

Rebalancing and costs. Monthly. The turnover cap applies to chosen turnover, measured from the portfolio left after last month's returns with leavers sold and entrants bought at their benchmark weight. Returns are gross of fees. A trading cost of 10 basis points per unit of one-way turnover is charged where stated, forced turnover included.

What the study cannot show. It is a simulation. The benchmark weights CRSP's list by full capitalisation, and the index weights by float. A GICS sub-industry is wider than a list of controversial weapons. Costs are proportional to turnover, with no market impact. The forecast is a covariance of daily returns times 21, so daily active returns correlated from one day to the next would raise the realised figure above it even with an exact covariance matrix, and the study measures the realised figure on monthly returns only. Sectors exist from July 1999, so the mandate starts there. No estimator's mean active return is statistically significant at the 5 per cent level, which over these 306 months would take one of about 35 basis points a year, so the study makes no claim about a return the mandate earns.

the cap-weighted universe, each stock's weight its market capitalisation over the universe's total.
active weight
a weight in the portfolio minus the same weight in the benchmark.
active return
the portfolio's return minus the benchmark's, each over the month's names with a return.
cap-weighted
weighted by market capitalisation. CRSP's value-weighted return of the list is cap-weighted too, over the members at the end of the month with ADRs left out and no delisting return.
value-weighted
cap-weighted.
basis point
a hundredth of a percentage point.
tracking error
the standard deviation of the monthly active return, times the square root of 12. The forecast is the one the month's covariance matrix implies for the month's active weights, the realised the measured one.
covariance matrix
the table of the stocks' variances and of their covariances with each other.
covariance estimator
a way of estimating the covariance matrix from three years of daily returns: the sample covariance, the Ledoit-Wolf estimator7, which shrinks it towards a scaled identity matrix, a factor model on French's five factors and momentum, and a principal-component model with the benchmark as its first factor. The last three are the structured estimators.
eigenvalue
the variance of one of the uncorrelated portfolios a covariance matrix splits into.
condition number
a covariance matrix's largest eigenvalue over its smallest.
band
the range of the bias statistic within which a forecast counts as right, 0.8 to 1.25, fixed before the run.
bias statistic
the standard deviation of each month's active return divided by that month's forecast, over the months with a forecast above one millionth: the standard deviation of the active return in units of each month's forecast. It is 1 when the forecasts are right on average, and it is not the realised tracking error divided by the mean forecast.
paired test
a test of the difference between two figures measured on the same months, here the variance test of Ledoit and Wolf (2011)8 of two realised tracking errors. Its 95 per cent interval comes from a circular block bootstrap, which takes new samples of the months in blocks of 12 consecutive months, and its p-value is the probability from the same bootstrap of a difference at least as far from zero if the two variances were equal. A difference whose interval excludes zero is statistically significant at that level.
p-value
the probability, from the bootstrap of a paired test, of a difference at least as far from zero as the one observed if the two variances were equal.
Holm's correction
an adjustment of the p-values of a family of comparisons for their number: the smallest is multiplied by the family's size, the next by one less, and each is at least the one before it.
mandate
the full set of constraints the portfolio obeys: long-only, each stock and each company outside the tilted sectors and the excluded sub-industries within 50 basis points of its benchmark weight, each untilted sector within 2 percentage points, chosen turnover capped, the tilt, two sub-industries at zero and a beta of one to the benchmark.
beta
the slope of a return on the benchmark's return.
tilt
every Energy and Utilities stock held between zero and half its benchmark weight.
constraint set
one of the cumulative sets C0 to C3: long-only, plus the bounds per stock, company and sector, plus the turnover cap, plus the rest of the mandate.
path
one covariance estimator under one constraint set at one turnover cap, run month by month from the benchmark.
turnover
a month's one-way trading as a share of the portfolio. Forced turnover is what index changes require, chosen turnover the rest.
turnover cap
the limit on chosen turnover, 2 per cent of the portfolio a month in the mandate, with 0.5, 1 and 4 per cent and no cap on the frontier.
reweighting
the turnover that following the benchmark's own changes in share counts and its dividends takes.
rebalancing
the optimiser's own moves. The overlap is the trades of one step that the next undoes.
Newey-West standard error
the standard error of a mean that allows each month to be correlated with the next twelve. A plain standard error makes no such allowance.
attribution
a split of the active return into parts that sum to it.
allocation
the part of the active return from holding a sector at another weight than the benchmark's: the sector's active weight times the benchmark sector's return less the benchmark's.
selection
the part from holding other stocks within a sector: the sector's benchmark weight times the portfolio sector's return less the benchmark sector's.
interaction
the sector's active weight times that same difference.
Unclassified
the twelfth sector, the members with no GICS sector, one to three securities a month.
factor
a common source of return: the market, size, value, profitability and investment factors of Fama and French (2015), and momentum.
loading
a stock's sensitivity to a factor, from a regression of its daily returns on the six factors over the three years before the month.
active exposure
the members' loadings on a factor weighted by the active weights. A factor's contribution is its active exposure times its return, the residual the active return less the contributions, and the factor share one minus the residual's variance over the active return's.
Sharpe ratio
in the card of Optimal versus naive diversification below, a portfolio's mean return above the one-month Treasury bill rate, over its standard deviation.
look-ahead
the use, in a decision dated at one time, of data published only later.
gate
a test of the data fixed in advance, which stops the study if not met.
expectation
a test of a result fixed in advance, reported as a finding whether met or not. One named for two or more estimators has one verdict, met only if it holds for each, a rule set on 12 November 2026 after the run of the mandate.

Other projects

Optimal versus naive diversification. Under an index-tracking mandate on French's 49 industries, five covariance estimators understate their own tracking error over 1979 to 2026: the realised active return is 22 to 61 per cent more volatile than forecast. The replication of DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009) reproduces 29 of the 30 Sharpe ratios that carry a band, 26 of 27 with the value-weighted market counted once. Page, dashboard, note, code

Climate risk as an equity signal. An ownership graph linking 328 ownership entities in the US and developed Europe, 302 of them listed companies with a price series, to 5,115 asset holdings, built from Global Energy Monitor's free tracker. Of 19 apparent ownership changes drawn at random and checked by hand, 12 had no corporate event behind them. Page, note, code

Volatility-managed portfolios: a replication, the real-time critique, and the months after the sample. Volatility estimation and position sizing on Ken French's equity factors, replicating Moreira and Muir (2017) and the real-time critique of Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan (2020). Scaled by the inverse of last month's realised variance, a factor's Sharpe ratio is higher than the original's by a margin that is statistically significant at the 5 per cent level for momentum alone when each of the six factors is tested on its own, and for no factor after Holm's correction for the six tests. In the 136 months after Moreira and Muir's sample no factor's difference is statistically significant, and the intervals are too wide to rule out the differences found on their sample. Page, dashboard, note, code

Rules-based portfolio. Runs a real-money portfolio under rules published before the first order, with every later change dated in the repository: two UCITS ETFs at 70/30, a monthly cycle with pre-trade checks, look-through exposure and ex-ante risk on a public dashboard.
